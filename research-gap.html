Scientific Motivation

Research Gaps & Our Response

Bridging the divide between static wildlife maps, sparse field observations, and everyday commuter travel decisions.

1. Static Hotspot Maps vs. Dynamic Movement

What is missing today

Existing conflict maps are predominantly static historical summaries. They show where past incidents or crop encounters have occurred over previous months or years, but cannot account for an animal's changing estimated position or growing spatial uncertainty over time.

How GAJA-SAVIYA responds

Components 2 and 3 introduce a time-varying position estimate and growing uncertainty region between sparse sightings, enabling dynamic road evaluations that adapt as time passes.

2. Restricted Access to Live Tracking Data

What is missing today

Physical GPS collar telemetry is costly, logistically difficult to maintain in harsh jungle conditions, and understandably restricted for undergraduate research teams due to wildlife safety and licensing constraints.

How GAJA-SAVIYA responds

A software-only approach using public data and clearly labelled synthetic trajectories with known ground truth, enabling reproducible and accessible algorithmic experimentation without capturing or collaring animals.

3. Fragmented, Disconnected Models

What is missing today

In academic literature, habitat suitability models, wildlife motion tracking filters, and roadway route planners are commonly treated as isolated, separate systems without a unified data pipeline.

How GAJA-SAVIYA responds

One integrated four-stage pipeline connecting landscape risk estimation, motion tracking, roadway classification, and route decision support into a cohesive software architecture.

4. Risk Shown but Not Translated into Safe Action

What is missing today

Conventional map interfaces often highlight danger zones or hotspot buffers, but leave the traveller without clear guidance on how to navigate around the hazard or how much detour time is required.

How GAJA-SAVIYA responds

Path Guard translates raw risk into practical action by presenting a side-by-side comparison of the ordinary shortest route versus a risk-weighted safer route, showing the exact distance trade-off and active threat areas.

Methodology

Evaluation Plan

Structured experimental protocols designed to assess each component against simulated ground truth.

Component How we will evaluate Measures & Metrics
1. Spatial Risk Surface Spatial cross-validation against held-out validation points. ROC-AUC, Precision-Recall, spatial agreement indices, and relative factor importance.
2. EKF Tracking Compare estimated coordinates against hidden synthetic trajectory ground truth. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) in metres, covariance uncertainty ellipse coverage percentage.
3. Road-Risk Assessment Compare road classifications against simulated dynamic risk corridor ground truth. True/false risky road segments, missed-risk segments, classification sensitivity.
4. Path Guard Routing Compare conventional shortest paths against risk-weighted routes across diverse simulated scenarios. Added travel distance (km), share of path through high-risk areas (%), calculation latency (ms).

Experimental Status Note

No empirical results are claimed yet. Results will be added after experiments are completed. This table outlines the intended evaluation methodology for the research framework.
