About GAJA-SAVIYA

An undergraduate research initiative developed by final-year Information Technology students at SLIIT.

We are final-year students reading for the Bachelor of Science (Hons) in Information Technology at the Faculty of Computing, Department of Information Technology, Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT), Malabe, Sri Lanka (October 2026). Our work on GAJA-SAVIYA explores how modern computational geometry, open geospatial data, and responsible simulation can assist rural communities facing human-elephant coexistence challenges.

Principles

Ethics & Responsible Use

Core commitments ensuring our research protects both rural communities and wildlife integrity.

  • No animals captured or collared:

    The project strictly uses software-based mathematical simulation and open landscape data; no wild animals were tracked with invasive tags or disturbed during this research.

  • Open data used under licence:

    All map infrastructure and highway networks are derived ethically from OpenStreetMap contributors under the Open Database Licence (ODbL).

  • Synthetic data always labelled:

    All wildlife tracks, simulated sightings, and uncertainty playback buffers are explicitly designated as synthetic to maintain full transparency.

  • Sensitive fine-scale wildlife locations are not published:

    To prevent harassment or poaching risks, high-resolution real-time wildlife coordinates are never exposed or made publicly discoverable.

  • Research decision support only:

    GAJA-SAVIYA is designed as an educational and advisory prototype, not a replacement for the Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) or statutory emergency response teams.

GAJA-SAVIYA
